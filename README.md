Through these mathematical projects I aimed to deepen my knowledge of how mathematics and coding interact together in computer programmes.

My first project (heat.py) aimed to solve the partial differential equation 'the heat equation'. Firstly, I researched and learned how to solve this equation and read a proof on how the equation is derived. To understand this proof I utilised knowledge from my A-Level Maths and A-Level Further Maths specifications on differentiation and differential equations. Once I reached the practical stage of this project, I first solved a general form of the one dimensional heat equation and this was quite challenging. Normally I would be given boundary conditions to be able to get an exact answer for the heat distribution, however in this equation I used a more general solution; in which there was a heat source on one side of a rod with thermal diffusivity alpha and length L. This step gave me an infinite sum which presented a challenge as I was unable to use it to find an exact answer. To overcome this problem, I programmed the sum into python and approximated the answer with a large value instead of infinity as the upper bound (this is because you cannot put infinity into python). At this point the equation was solved and the programme was written, however I wanted to explore further and took some time substituting in values to see how the temperature at different points changed based on the length, thermal diffusivity and temperature of the heat source. I found that as the length increases, overall temperature decreases and that when thermal diffusivity increases the temperature at different points increases. Overall, I feel I became more secure in my knowledge of partial differential equations.

In my next project (prime.py) I aimed to programme Willan's Nth Prime Formula. I was interested in this as it was another infinite sum which I did not know how to calculate and I was curious about the accuracy of the result. Initially I researched the formula online and then programmed it into python and then I inputted different numbers for N to see the accuracy of the result. I found that the result was accurate however, the programme was very time inefficient due to nested iteration which has a time complexity of O(2^n). I was unable to find a solution for this but I was interested to find that there was a formula that would give the nth prime.
